Privacy

The short version: Betar collects nothing. There is no server, and nothing written in the app ever leaves the phones it travels between.

What Betar does not have

No account

No signup, no email, no phone number, no login.

No analytics

No usage tracking, no crash reports sent anywhere.

No advertising

Nothing to sell, nothing to target.

No internet permission

The app does not request it at all. There is nowhere for your data to go.

What is stored, and where

A device identity and its keys are generated on first launch and kept only on the device, encrypted at rest. Messages you send or receive are kept on your device and on the devices that carry them until they reach where they are going, or until you delete them. Nothing is uploaded to a server, because there is no server.

What nearby phones can and cannot see

A nearby phone can see that your device is present and can pass along messages addressed through it, the same way any phone in the mesh does. It cannot read a message that is not addressed to it: messages are encrypted for their recipient before they ever leave your phone.

Permissions, and why

Betar asks for Bluetooth and Wi-Fi Direct permissions to find and talk to nearby phones, and location permission only where the operating system requires it for that same nearby discovery, not to track where you are. It does not ask for internet access, contacts, or anything unrelated to passing messages between nearby devices.

Children

Betar does not collect personal information from anyone, including children, because it does not collect personal information at all.

How to check this yourself

Every claim on this page is verifiable in the source code, and the app requests no internet permission at all, which anyone can confirm by reading its manifest.
