About Betar

Betar is communication and safety infrastructure that works with no SIM, no internet, no router and no cell tower. It is built on Project Mesh, the open protocol and Rust core that makes it work.

The name

Betar (বেতার) is Bengali for wireless: literally be- (without) and tar (wire).

It honours Jagadish Chandra Bose. In November 1895, in Calcutta, he sent radio waves through two walls and a seated man's body to ring a bell and set off gunpowder in the next room, a public demonstration of wireless transmission before Marconi's own. He worked at millimetre wavelengths a century before ordinary phones got there, and he refused to patent any of it because he believed the work belonged to everyone.

He demonstrated wireless transmission publicly before Marconi and gave the work away unpatented. Whether he "invented radio" outright is genuinely contested, and this project makes no claim beyond what the historical record supports.

Betar is open source under AGPL-3.0. Same idea, roughly a hundred and thirty years later.

Betar and Project Mesh

Project Mesh is the name of the open protocol and the Rust core underneath: identity, encryption, message routing, the pieces that make phone to phone delivery work without a server. Betar is the application built on top of it, and the name most people will actually use.

Both live in the same public repository, so anyone can read, build and check the whole thing rather than trusting a claim on a page.
